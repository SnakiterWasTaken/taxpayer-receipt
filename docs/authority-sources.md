# Official government responsibilities — evidence log

The explorer's **Who is responsible?** disclosures describe publicly documented **legal and administrative duties**, not the share of total spending that any authority controls. COFOG `GF04`, `GF05` and `GF09` describe broad international **purposes of spending**. The reviewed Czech cases below describe only one named service within each function. They cannot be generalized to all expenses inside those functions.

## Source and year rule

- A published authority record identifies `country`, `COFOG category`, narrow service `scope`, `asOfYear`, institutional roles, exceptions and direct original public-government sources.
- Reviewed **2026** legal duties are shown as current published responsibilities when a visitor selects Czechia **even if the Eurostat expenditure year is 2024**. The page explicitly warns that the duties **have not been historically verified for the selected spending year**.
- Blank, unknown, or unreviewed categories and countries display: **No verified official public data available here for this country and spending category yet.** This means the project has not verified an official source; **not** that no public law or competent authority exists.
- The project does not interpret a government sector reporting expenditure as evidence of power to set policy, approve spending, or control the funds; those are different claims.
- Future historical records must use official in-force law versions for their year, not silently project 2026 responsibilities backwards.

## Verified Czech examples (official sources only)

### GF04 Economic affairs: public road ownership and upkeep

**Official law:** [Czech Road Act 13/1997, §9, e-Sbírka](https://e-sbirka.gov.cz/sb/1997/13). It assigns ownership of motorways and first-class roads to the **state**, second- and third-class roads to the **region**, and local roads to the **municipality**. Road owners must undertake inspection, maintenance and repairs, but may use a controlled road manager.

**Municipal budget authority:** [Ministry of the Interior, powers of municipal authorities](https://mv.gov.cz/organy-obce-a-jejich-kompetence). A local elected council approves the municipal budget. This does not establish a proportion of **total GF04** controlled by the council.

**Not covered:** vehicle subsidies, energy, national economic policy, public transport contracts, intergovernmental transfers or actual spending amounts per road owner.

### GF05 Environmental protection: municipal waste collection

**Official administrative guide:** [Czech Public Administration Portal, municipal waste-system obligations](https://portal.gov.cz/sluzby-vs/svoz-odpadu-S44747). The municipality must accept local household waste, arrange separate collection locations and may adopt a municipal ordinance specifying the system.

**Municipal approval:** [Ministry of the Interior, municipal powers](https://mv.gov.cz/organy-obce-a-jejich-kompetence). The elected council approves the municipal budget and adopts local generally binding ordinances.

**Not covered:** the whole environment budget, biodiversity, pollution controls outside the municipal service, contracts with specific collectors, or the proportion funded by fees versus transfers.

### GF09 Education: municipal preschool and compulsory primary education

**Official law:** [Education Act 561/2004, §178, e-Sbírka](https://e-sbirka.gov.cz/sb/2004/561). Municipalities are obliged to ensure compulsory school attendance can be fulfilled, whether by establishing their own primary schools or arranging it through another municipality/association. The law also imposes duties concerning preschool availability.

**Municipal budget authority:** [Ministry of the Interior, municipal powers](https://mv.gov.cz/organy-obce-a-jejich-kompetence). The elected municipal council approves local budgets and may establish or discontinue municipal organisations.

**Not covered:** secondary schools, universities, teacher payroll allocation, national educational lawmaking, individual institution accounts or complete GF09 spending authority.

## Not yet validated

GF01 General public services, GF02 Defence, GF03 Public order and safety, GF06 Housing and community amenities, GF07 Health, GF08 Recreation/culture/religion and GF10 Social protection **have no reviewed nationwide authority mapping** in this version. Nor do other EU countries. Show the explicit no-data status rather than inferred ministry names. Even for GF04/GF05/GF09, unverified *parts* of each category remain clearly excluded.

## Next evidence-driven extensions

1. Expand each currently reviewed category only when new official material explicitly documents the relevant authority and service.
2. Audit historical effective law versions for each selectable expenditure year, including institutional changes.
3. Add source-dated mappings for other EU countries through their public legislation, ministries and municipal/regional authorities.
4. Investigate independently sourced expenditure by government subsector only after resolving consolidation and intergovernmental transfers; do not confuse accounting units with decision-making power.
5. Keep citations and responsibility labels translatable without modifying the official-language legal links.

No local-government monetary allocation or percentage is asserted in the first release.


## 27-country reporting supplement (October 2026)

The new `src/sector-spending.js` snapshot uses official Eurostat Table 11 subsector values to show **where expenditure is recorded**; see the [EU27 sweep and national budget-source audit](eu27-authority-sweep.md). The categorisation identifies `S1311` central government, `S1312` regional/state government, `S1313` local government and `S1314` social-security funds. It is **not an institution-level management hierarchy**, and subsection TE values are non-additive to national consolidated S13 TE. Actual controlling institutions remain unknown until separate official law, approval records, public accounts and transfers reconcile.
