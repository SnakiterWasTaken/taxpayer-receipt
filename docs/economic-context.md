# Historical economic context: definitions and limits

These official economic trends complement fiscal spending; they do not establish a causal relationship with public policies. Missing values are not inferred, extrapolated or treated as zero.

| Indicator | Eurostat source | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Inflation | https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/databrowser/view/prc_hicp_ainr/default/table?lang=en | Annual-average HICP all-items percentage change, not December-on-December inflation or an individual household's inflation |
| Minimum wage | https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/databrowser/view/earn_mw_cur/default/table?lang=en | 1 January gross monthly **statutory national** minimum wage. Five EU members (AT, DK, FI, IT, SE) have no national statutory wage floor and generally use collective agreements |
| Real disposable income per person | https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/databrowser/view/tepsr_wc310/default/table?lang=en | **Plain explanation:** the country's average household income per resident, after taxes and transfers, adjusted for changing consumer prices. The index is **100 in 2008**: 120 means 20% higher than 2008 *in that country*, not €120. This is a national average, not the median; it does not compare income levels across countries. |

Wage movement compares 1 January with the previous 1 January, and is nominal. Inflation is an annual average; do not treat the two observation windows as equivalent. Disposable income reflects household disposable resources per person after consumption-price changes, not the median employee's real wage or individual purchasing power.

A calendar year's spending and outcomes may reflect long policy and economic lags. Historical datasets can be revised. Eurostat's national-currency wage series across a currency conversion needs special interpretation. Further research may add median equivalised income, housing overburden, in-work poverty and debt, with separate denominators and source provenance.

The deploy action refreshes these series automatically, checks 2024 country coverage and rejects incomplete source extracts. Country-year gaps are displayed as unavailable. See the individual source records in the generated economic-context.json for data provenance.


See the [plain-language glossary](glossary.html#household-income-index) for the worked 100 → 120 example, the meaning of real versus nominal, and why this index cannot rank different countries by living standards. Eurostat defines the series as household and household-serving non-profit disposable income divided by consumer price change and total resident population, rebased to 2008=100.
