This explorer is intentionally conservative: only complete, reconciled official statistics are used. Country-specific calculator rules and source-backed notes appear in the corresponding country view.

What data do we use?

All displayed budget expenditure comes from Eurostat's gov_10a_exp dataset ↗. The figures refer to total annual expenditure across general government (S13), including local and regional authorities and applicable social security subsectors, measured using the ten top-level COFOG functions (GF01–GF10).

How are proportions calculated?

Each category's published expenditure is divided by the sum of the ten categories for the same country and year. These ten figures must be present and reconcile to the independent official TOTAL value within 0.5%. If a category is missing or the total fails this test, that country-year is not displayed.

If you enter a tax amount, the application multiplies that amount by each spending proportion. Where an independently validated national calculator exists, visitors may enter salary instead of a known annual tax amount. No payments are literally traced. The input is intentionally kept locally in your browser.

What does “your tax money” mean?

It is an illustrative comparison, not a literal allocation. Governments spend revenue gathered from multiple tax sources and non-tax income, and also borrow. Tax revenue is generally not earmarked to correspond with any particular individual's services or expenditures.

National tax calculators

Tax rules, social insurance, eligibility and tax-year-specific thresholds differ by country. Supported national calculators disclose their assumptions and official sources on the main explorer when that country is selected. Countries without validated calculations allow manual entry of a known annual tax amount.

Historical economic context

Alongside spending data, the explorer displays independent Eurostat series for annual-average HICP inflation, January statutory national minimum wage, and real gross disposable household income per person (index 2008=100). These datasets have different observation dates and are not automatically attributable to fiscal policies. Missing observations are not imputed; the real-income index is only compared within a country over time. See detailed indicator definitions and sources ↗.

Source, attribution and reuse
