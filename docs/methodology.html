This explorer is intentionally conservative: only complete, reconciled official statistics are used. It now includes a limited Czech employee tax calculator for 2024–2026.

What data do we use?

All displayed budget expenditure comes from Eurostat's gov_10a_exp dataset ↗. The figures refer to total annual expenditure across general government (S13), including local and regional authorities and applicable social security subsectors, measured using the ten top-level COFOG functions (GF01–GF10).

How are proportions calculated?

Each category's published expenditure is divided by the sum of the ten categories for the same country and year. These ten figures must be present and reconcile to the independent official TOTAL value within 0.5%. If a category is missing or the total fails this test, that country-year is not displayed.

If you enter a tax amount, the application multiplies that amount by each spending proportion. For Czechia, an optional salary estimate is available; for other countries, the visitor supplies a known tax amount. No payments are literally traced. The input is intentionally kept locally in your browser.

What does “your tax money” mean?

It is an illustrative comparison, not a literal allocation. Governments spend revenue gathered from multiple tax sources and non-tax income, and also borrow. Tax revenue is generally not earmarked to correspond with any particular individual's services or expenditures.

Czech employment calculator (2024–2026)

For Czechia, enter a full-year salary in CZK for a standard employee at one employer. The model distributes gross income over twelve payslips, calculates employee social insurance (7.1%), employee health insurance (about 4.5% via one third of 13.5% combined premiums), and progressive income tax (15%/23%) with the annual basic credit of CZK 30,840. It separately displays employer contributions, which do not reduce employee take-home pay.

Year-specific higher-rate tax thresholds are CZK 1,582,812 (2024), CZK 1,676,052 (2025) and CZK 1,762,812 (2026). Social insurance bases are capped at CZK 2,110,416, CZK 2,234,736 and CZK 2,350,416, respectively. Figures use monthly contribution rounding and annual income-tax settlement assumptions.

Limitations: this is a simplified full-time, year-round, regular-salary model. It does not cover OSVČ, DPP/DPČ, partial-year benefits, deductions, different employers, exceptional income, part-time or special minimum health-insurance contributions. Salaries below the annualized full-time minimum are intentionally rejected rather than silently omitting minimum-health rules. Do not rely on estimates for actual payroll or tax filing.

Household tax reliefs

Dependent-child credit (full year) is CZK 15,204 for the first, 22,320 for the second and 27,840 for the third or each further child; a qualifying child ZTP/P card doubles that child's amount. The other parent must not claim the same child for the same period. A child's legal dependent status requires additional conditions.

The full-year spouse/eligible registered partner credit is CZK 24,840 (CZK 49,680 if spouse has ZTP/P) only where you cohabit with the spouse and a qualifying dependent child under three and the spouse's own annual income does not exceed CZK 68,000. The full-year estimate assumes the conditions are met at the start of each month. Disability grades I/II and III allow annual credits of CZK 2,520 and CZK 5,040 respectively. Eligible taxpayer ZTP/P adds CZK 16,140 annually.

Child tax bonus: only the unused child credit can be refundable, with six-times-minimum-wage annual income threshold (2024: CZK 113,400; 2025: 124,800; 2026: 134,400), and a minimum CZK 100 annual bonus. The bonus appears separately as a transfer that increases net pay; it is not counted as tax contributed. Other credits are nonrefundable. Monthly advance comparisons assume only the basic credit was applied during the year.

Important: selections represent self-declared eligibility for all 12 months. Partial-year births, changing eligibility, partner income details, split child relief and nonresident exceptions are unsupported. Czech Financial Administration's current employee guidance ↗ explains the qualifications.

What about salary calculations in other countries?

National payroll systems differ by deductions, credits, tax residence and household situation. Other countries use a known-tax-amount input until country- and tax-year-specific calculations have been independently validated.
