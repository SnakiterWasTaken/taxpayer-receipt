PUBLIC ACCOUNTING

Pensions deserve
a clearer breakdown.

Separate reported pension-related expenditure from the accounting conventions used to finance public-sector retirement systems.

1. Comparable public expenditure: COFOG

The explorer uses Eurostat's general-government S13 expenditure. Its GF10 Social protection category contains smaller groups including GF1002 Old age and GF1003 Survivors. The two are shown within, not on top of, GF10. These groups cover more than cash pension payments and may not include all national supplementary schemes.

2. France: public-sector pension financing in ministry budgets

The official 2027 annex to France's draft budget says that EUR 28.5 billion, or 32%, of the Education Ministry's planned credits are directed to the CAS Pensions account. It identifies EUR 25.1 billion in employer contributions and EUR 3.4 billion in employee contributions. These amounts describe financing flows inside ministry budget presentation, not an additional EUR 28.5 billion beyond pensions already reported elsewhere.

The annex explicitly states that the ministry-budget contribution convention does not directly determine ESA national-accounts international comparisons. Mixing the two without a reconciliation would create misleading totals.

3. The independent accounting criticism

The Institut des politiques publiques (2025) argues that government employers' unusually high rates incorporate an implicit subsidy related to demographic imbalance and other pension obligations. It proposes identifying that subsidy separately. Its alternative employer contribution rate of 34.7% concerns 2020, while its educational spending illustration concerns 2023—not the Eurostat year selected on our website.

4. A different total: DREES

The DREES 2024 report records EUR 426.7 billion in old-age and survivors' benefits across a wider set of social-protection schemes that includes certain private arrangements. That number is not interchangeable with Eurostat's S13 COFOG data.

5. What we will investigate next

A future view may show original ministry appropriations alongside a clearly labelled, nonofficial pension-financing adjustment. It will require matched years, exact source line items, a documented crosswalk and conservation tests. We will not present alternative conventions as historical facts or double-count pension payments.
