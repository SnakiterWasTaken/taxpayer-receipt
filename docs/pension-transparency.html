EU-WIDE METHODOLOGY

Pensions in the accounts.
Understand the scope.

The same official definitions help citizens compare countries without double-counting benefits or financing flows.

1. Comparable public expenditure: COFOG

The explorer uses Eurostat general-government S13 expenditure. Its GF10 Social protection category contains GF1002 Old age and GF1003 Survivors. These groups are already inside GF10; displaying them separately does not increase the reported total.

2. Pension-related functions are not identical to cash pensions

The old-age function can include in-kind services and administration, while survivors may include more than retirement benefits. Some national pension arrangements may not lie inside the general-government statistical perimeter. Do not assume that this equals all retirement payments in every national scheme.

3. Expenditure is different from financing

Ministry budgets, pension-fund financing accounts, and consolidated national-accounts expenditure have different accounting purposes. Employer contributions within a ministry budget should not simply be added to pension benefits already recorded elsewhere: without a reconciled source-to-source bridge, that can double-count flows.

4. Country-specific accounting explanations

Reviewed national accounting notes appear on the main explorer only when their country is selected. Each note cites its own source year and scope. A national draft budget and Eurostat actual spending for another year are not directly interchangeable. A country without reviewed notes is not assumed to have no accounting differences.

5. What your proportional receipt does—and does not—mean

An illustrative allocation applies observed spending shares to an entered or estimated tax amount. It does not trace your particular tax payments, prove earmarking, or determine whether public policy caused a change in household living standards.
