FRANCE · NATIONAL ACCOUNTING NOTE

Pensions and ministry
budget conventions.

A national budget presentation, distinct from Eurostat's cross-country public-expenditure statistics.

The government's proposed 2027 figures

The official draft 2027 pensions budget annex indicates that €28.5 billion, or 32%, of the Education Ministry's planned credits go to the CAS Pensions account. It separates €25.1 billion of employer contributions and €3.4 billion of employee contributions. These are pension-financing flows in a ministry's planned budget, not additional spending to add to Eurostat's old-age figures.

The independent criticism

An Institut des politiques publiques study (2025) argues that exceptionally high state-employer contribution rates partly represent an implicit subsidy to pension financing, rather than ordinary labour costs. Its illustrative alternative rate of 34.7% refers to 2020, and its education-budget comparison refers to 2023: neither should be transplanted into the proposed 2027 accounts without new reconciliation.

Why budget reclassification is not national-accounts reclassification

The official annex distinguishes ministry-budget contributions from ESA national-accounting imputed social contributions. A hypothetical alternative ministry-budget presentation does not directly alter Eurostat's consolidated COFOG categories. Any bridge must match years, institutional coverage, counterparty transactions and source line items, and preserve accounting identities.

All-scheme pension totals use yet another denominator

DREES reports €426.7 billion in 2024 old-age and survivors' benefits across a broader set of public and some private social-protection schemes. It is not directly interchangeable with Eurostat consolidated S13 government expenditure. DREES official source ↗.
